---
title: PharmAMP, developability metrics for designed antimicrobial peptides
date: "2026-09"
summary: Aggregation-propensity and solubility metrics compatible with seqme, checked against measured safety data for designed peptides, with the limits stated.
topic: release
---

In September 2026 I released PharmAMP, a small open-source package that computes developability metrics for antimicrobial peptides designed by generative models. It is released under the MIT licence and its documentation states what it can and cannot tell you.

Generative models can now propose thousands of candidate antimicrobial peptides, and the proposals are judged mainly on predicted antimicrobial activity. A peptide that is active but aggregates in solution, is hard to dissolve, or damages human cells will not become a medicine. Developability is the name for those practical properties. PharmAMP provides two of them, an aggregation-propensity metric and a solubility metric, in a form that works with seqme, an evaluation library for sequence generative models, so that the metrics can be used in the same pipeline as other evaluations.

What I most wanted was a check against real measurements. A metric that is plausible on paper may track nothing in a laboratory. I tested the two metrics against published measurements for 217 peptides generated by an antimicrobial peptide model, taken from a recent preprint on designing antimicrobials with programmable mechanism and safety. The metrics were not fitted to these data, so the comparison is an independent test.

The result is mixed, and the README says so. Both metrics correlate with measured cytotoxicity in the expected direction. Aggregation propensity correlates at about −0.20 and solubility at about +0.19, in 215 peptides with data, and both are statistically significant. Neither correlates significantly with hemolysis, but about half of the hemolysis values are recorded only as above the assay ceiling, so that absence of correlation carries real uncertainty. The most surprising finding is that aggregation propensity correlates negatively with the measured β-sheet content of the peptides, at about −0.40. A metric built on the idea of amyloid-like aggregation should go the other way. The likely explanation is that antimicrobial peptides are mostly helical and amphipathic, so the metric's hydrophobic-patch heuristic reflects membrane-disruptive character and not β-sheet aggregation. I wrote that into the documentation and noted that the metric probably deserves a new name or a rework.

I reported the censored fraction beside each correlation, since a result with half its values censored is a different kind of result from one with none. The supplementary data file is distributed under a licence that does not allow redistribution, so the repository does not include it, and anyone repeating the benchmark has to obtain it from the source.

I released the package at this stage deliberately. Small developability tools tend to appear with claims that are never checked, and a measured comparison, even a partial one, is more useful to a reader than a clean story. The comparison is also available as a report on the Model validation page.

PharmAMP belongs to the same group of projects as CYPMode and CEREBRO-X: open code, acceptance criteria stated in advance where possible, and an account of the failures next to the successes.
